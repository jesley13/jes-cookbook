---
title: Kerala Pork Fry
---

Serves 6

Best cut

Use 1 kg pork with some fat attached — pork belly, shoulder, or a mix.

Don't use very lean pork. The fat renders during the final frying and is an important part of the flavour.

Ingredients
For cooking the pork
1 kg pork, cut into 1½-inch pieces
1½ tsp salt
½ tsp turmeric
1½ tsp freshly crushed black pepper
1½ tbsp ginger-garlic paste
2 green chillies, slit
1 medium onion, sliced
1 tsp fennel seeds
½ cup water
For the fry
2 large onions, thinly sliced
8–10 cloves garlic, chopped
1-inch ginger, finely chopped
3–4 green chillies, slit
2 sprigs curry leaves
2–3 tbsp coconut oil
1½ tsp black pepper, coarsely crushed
Roasted spice mix
2 tsp coriander powder
1½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
½–1 tsp hot chilli powder
1 tsp black pepper
½ tsp fennel powder
¼ tsp cumin powder
¼ tsp turmeric
Whole spices
1-inch cinnamon
4 cloves
3 cardamoms
1 star anise — optional
1. Cook the pork

Put the pork into a pressure cooker with:

salt
turmeric
1½ tsp pepper
ginger-garlic paste
green chillies
sliced onion
fennel seeds
½ cup water

Mix well.

Pressure cook:

Full flame until the first whistle → medium flame for about 15 minutes.

Switch off and let the pressure release naturally.

Check the pork

It should be tender but still holding its shape.

If it's still firm, give it another 5 minutes under pressure.

Keep the cooking liquid. You'll use it while frying.

2. Roast the spices

This makes a big difference.

In a dry pan over low-medium heat, add:

coriander powder
Kashmiri chilli powder
hot chilli powder
black pepper
fennel powder
cumin powder

Roast for about 30–45 seconds.

Don't burn them.

Remove immediately.

3. Start the pork fry

Use a wide, heavy pan.

Heat:

2–3 tbsp coconut oil

Add:

cinnamon
cloves
cardamom
star anise

Fry for about 20 seconds.

Add:

sliced onions
ginger
garlic
green chillies
curry leaves

Fry on medium heat for 8–10 minutes.

You want the onions deep golden brown.

This is where much of the flavour comes from.

4. Add the roasted spices

Lower the heat.

Add your roasted spice mixture.

Stir continuously for about 30 seconds.

If it looks like the spices are catching, immediately add 2–3 tbsp of the pork cooking liquid.

5. Add the pork

Add all the cooked pork.

Mix thoroughly.

Now add about ½ cup of the reserved pork stock.

Cook uncovered on medium heat for 10–15 minutes, stirring occasionally.

The liquid should gradually disappear.

6. The important final frying

Once most of the liquid has evaporated, reduce to medium-low heat.

Continue frying for another 10–15 minutes.

You'll see the pork becoming:

dark → glossy → slightly crisp around the edges.

The rendered pork fat and coconut oil will coat the pieces.

Add the remaining 1½ tsp crushed black pepper during the final 5 minutes.

Add another handful of curry leaves.

Don't rush this stage.

This slow frying is what turns pressure-cooked pork into proper Kerala pork fry.

🌶️ Final taste

You want:

peppery + spicy + savoury + aromatic + slightly smoky

If needed:

More salt → add carefully
More heat → add hot chilli powder
More pepper → freshly crushed black pepper
Too dry → a tablespoon or two of pork stock

I wouldn't add sugar or ketchup.

⭐ My preferred version

For a really good Kerala-style result, I'd use:

1 kg fatty pork + coconut oil + plenty of curry leaves + roasted coriander/fennel/pepper + slow final frying.

And I'd let some pieces get slightly crisp and dark while keeping others tender. That contrast is what makes the dish particularly good.

Serve with
Kerala parotta
Appam
Kallappam
Puttu
Ghee rice
Plain rice

For me, pork fry + Kerala parotta + a simple onion/cucumber salad is the best combination.
