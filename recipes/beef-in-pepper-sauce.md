1 kg beef | Serves 6

Ingredients

A. For pressure-cooking the beef

1 kg beef, cut into 1½-inch pieces1½ tsp salt1 tsp turmeric powder1½ tsp freshly crushed black pepper1½ tbsp ginger-garlic paste1 medium onion, sliced2 green chillies, slit — optional½ tsp fennel seeds1 cup water

B. For the pepper sauce

2 large onions, thinly sliced10–12 cloves garlic, finely chopped1-inch piece ginger, finely chopped3–4 tsp coarsely crushed black pepper1 tsp freshly ground black pepper2 tbsp butter2 tbsp oil1½ tbsp soy sauce1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce1 tbsp tomato ketchup1 tsp vinegar1–1½ cups reserved beef cooking stock½ cup fresh creamSalt, only if required

Optional

1 green capsicum, sliced150 g mushrooms, slicedCurry leaves — a handful

1. Pressure-cook the beef

Put into the pressure cooker:

beef + salt + turmeric + 1½ tsp pepper + ginger-garlic paste + onion + green chillies + fennel + 1 cup water.

Mix well.

Cook on full flame until the first whistle.

Then reduce to medium flame and cook for 15–20 minutes.

Switch off and allow the pressure to release naturally.

Check the beef

Open the cooker and test a piece.

It should be tender enough to bite easily but not falling apart.

If it is still chewy, add a little water and pressure-cook for another 5 minutes on medium.

Important: Keep the cooking liquid. Don't throw it away.

2. Start the pepper sauce

Heat a large, wide pan.

Add:

2 tbsp oil2 tbsp butter

Add the sliced onions.

Cook on medium heat for 8–10 minutes.

You want them deeply golden, not merely translucent. This is an important part of the flavour.

Add:

chopped gingerchopped garliccurry leaves, if using

Fry for 2–3 minutes.

3. Add the pepper

Add:

3–4 tsp coarsely crushed black pepper1 tsp finely ground black pepper

Fry for only 30–45 seconds.

Don't burn the pepper.

The aroma should become very strong at this point.

4. Build the sauce

Add:

1½ tbsp soy sauce1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce1 tbsp ketchup1 tsp vinegar

Mix well.

Now add 1–1½ cups of the reserved beef stock.

Bring to a good simmer.

Cook for 5–7 minutes.

Taste.

You want:

salty + savoury + strong black pepper + slight tang

—not sweet.

If necessary, add a little salt.

5. Add the beef

Add the pressure-cooked beef.

Mix thoroughly so the sauce coats every piece.

Simmer uncovered for 8–10 minutes on medium-low heat.

This is where the dish really comes together.

The sauce should reduce and become thick enough to cling to the beef.

If it gets too dry, add a little more beef stock.

6. Add cream

Reduce the heat to low.

Add:

½ cup fresh cream.

Mix gently.

Simmer for only 2–3 minutes.

Don't boil it aggressively.

Finally add 1 tbsp butter and mix through.

7. Optional vegetables

If you want a more substantial restaurant-style version, add:

Capsicum

Add 1 sliced capsicum during the final 5 minutes.

Mushrooms

Add 150 g sliced mushrooms when you add the beef.

I personally prefer mushrooms + beef + pepper sauce.

🌶️ How peppery should it be?

For 1 kg beef, I'd start with:

4–5 tsp total black pepper.

That's a proper pepper sauce.

If your family likes it really peppery, go up to 6 tsp, but add the last teaspoon near the end rather than all at once.

Freshly coarsely crushed pepper makes a big difference. Don't use only packaged fine pepper powder.

🍽️ What I'd serve with it

For six adults, my first choice would be:

Beef Pepper Sauce + Kerala parotta + onion salad

or

Beef Pepper Sauce + ghee rice + simple vegetable side

If you're serving it with rice, keep the sauce slightly more liquid. For parotta, reduce it further so it becomes thick and coats the beef.

One final tip

After the beef has been pressure-cooked, don't fry it hard again. The pressure cooker has already tenderised it. The second cooking stage is primarily about reducing the stock and allowing the beef to absorb the pepper sauce. That will give you tender beef rather than tough, overcooked pieces.
