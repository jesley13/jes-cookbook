---
title: 🥥 Kerala Parippu Payasam with Jaggery
---

Serves 6
Ingredients
150 g cherupayar parippu (moong dal)
300 g jaggery — start with 250 g and adjust
400 ml thick coconut milk
400 ml thin coconut milk or water
2 tbsp ghee
25 g cashews
20 g grated coconut
1 tsp cardamom powder
¼ tsp dry ginger powder
Small pinch of salt
1 tbsp raisins — optional
Important for the "sugar-safe" version

I'd use 250 g jaggery initially, rather than the usual 350–400+ g. For six people, that gives roughly 40 g jaggery per serving before accounting for the natural carbohydrates in the dal/coconut milk.

Jaggery is still sugar from a blood-glucose perspective, so portion size matters.

1. Roast the dal

Dry-roast the moong dal in a heavy pan over medium-low heat for 4–5 minutes.

You want it lightly golden and aromatic.

Don't brown it heavily.

2. Cook the dal

Wash the roasted dal.

Add to a pressure cooker with about 400 ml water.

Cook until completely soft.

You can use your usual pressure-cooker approach:

Full flame → first whistle → low/medium flame for 5 minutes → switch off.

Let the pressure release naturally.

Mash the dal lightly.

Don't make it completely smooth—you want a little texture.

3. Prepare the jaggery

Put 250 g jaggery + about 100 ml water in a saucepan.

Heat until completely dissolved.

Strain through a fine sieve to remove any impurities.

Return the syrup to the pan.

4. Combine

Add the jaggery syrup to the cooked dal.

Mix well.

Cook on medium-low heat for 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally.

Add:

¼ tsp dry ginger
1 tsp cardamom
tiny pinch of salt
Taste now.

If you want it sweeter, add another 25–50 g jaggery.

I'd stop around 300 g total for this recipe.

5. Add thin coconut milk

Add 400 ml thin coconut milk.

Simmer gently for 5 minutes.

Don't boil aggressively.

6. Add thick coconut milk

Lower the heat completely.

Add:

400 ml thick coconut milk.

Stir gently.

Heat for 2–3 minutes, but do not bring it to a rolling boil.

Turn off the heat.

7. Finish with ghee

Heat 2 tbsp ghee in a small pan.

Fry:

cashews
grated coconut
raisins, if using

until lightly golden.

Pour everything into the payasam.

Stir gently.

⭐ My preferred balance

For your first attempt I'd use:

150 g dal + 275 g jaggery + 800 ml coconut milk

That should give you a properly sweet Kerala payasam, but noticeably less sweet than the conventional versions.

If you're specifically trying to keep the glucose impact lower, I'd serve small bowls—around 100–120 ml per person, rather than large dessert portions.

And I would choose parippu payasam over palada/semiya payasam for this purpose: the dal gives you substantially more protein and fibre, while the latter are predominantly starch + milk + sugar.
