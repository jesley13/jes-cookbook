---
title: Croissants
---

Makes 12
Ingredients
Dough
500 g bread flour or strong maida
60 g sugar
10 g salt
10 g instant dry yeast
250 ml cold milk
100 ml cold water
30 g unsalted butter, softened
Butter block
250 g unsalted butter
1 tbsp flour
Egg wash
1 egg
1 tbsp milk
Pinch of salt
1. Make the dough

Mix together:

500 g flour
60 g sugar
10 g salt
10 g instant yeast

Add:

250 ml cold milk
100 ml cold water
30 g butter

Mix until you get a rough dough.

Knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic.

Don't make it excessively soft or sticky.

Shape into a rectangle, cover tightly and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, preferably overnight.

2. Make the butter block

Take 250 g cold unsalted butter.

Place it between two sheets of baking paper.

Using a rolling pin, flatten and shape it into approximately:

20 × 15 cm rectangle

The butter should be cold but pliable.

It should have roughly the same firmness as the chilled dough.

Refrigerate until needed.

3. Enclose the butter

Roll the chilled dough into approximately:

40 × 20 cm

Place the butter block in the centre.

Fold the dough over it completely and seal the edges.

You now have a butter-filled dough rectangle.

4. First fold

Roll the dough into approximately:

60 × 20 cm

Don't press excessively hard; you're trying to stretch the dough while keeping the butter intact.

Fold it into thirds like a letter:

bottom third → centre → top third over everything

Wrap and refrigerate for 30–45 minutes.

5. Second fold

Rotate the dough 90°.

Roll again to approximately 60 × 20 cm.

Fold into thirds again.

Wrap and refrigerate for another 30–45 minutes.

That's enough.

For home croissants, two good letter folds give you plenty of layers without making the process unnecessarily difficult.

6. Roll the final dough

Roll the chilled dough into a large rectangle approximately:

60 × 25 cm

Aim for about 3–4 mm thickness.

If the dough becomes soft or the butter starts feeling greasy, stop and refrigerate it for 15–20 minutes.

This is one of the most important rules of croissant making.

7. Cut the croissants

Trim the edges so you have a neat rectangle.

Cut into 12 long triangles.

Each triangle should have:

base: about 10 cm
length: about 25 cm

Make a small 1–2 cm slit in the centre of the base.

8. Shape

Starting at the wide end, gently roll the triangle toward the tip.

Don't roll extremely tightly.

You want the layers to have room to expand.

Curve the ends slightly toward each other.

Place the croissants on a baking tray with the tip underneath.

9. Proof

This is the stage where patience matters.

Cover loosely and leave at approximately 24–26°C for 2–3 hours.

Don't rely purely on time.

Properly proofed croissants should:
look noticeably larger
feel very light
jiggle slightly when you gently shake the tray
show visible layers

Don't proof them somewhere very hot.

If the temperature gets much above 27–28°C, the butter can start melting out of the dough.

10. Egg wash

Mix:

1 egg
1 tbsp milk
pinch of salt

Brush gently over the croissants.

Try not to get egg wash on the cut edges — it can glue the layers together.

11. Bake

Preheat oven to 200°C.

Put the croissants in and immediately reduce to 190°C.

Bake for approximately 18–22 minutes.

They should be deep golden brown, not pale.

Don't take them out simply because the outside looks done — proper croissants need enough baking to cook the interior.

⭐ The three things that determine whether they work
1. Keep the butter cold

If butter melts during rolling, you lose the distinct layers.

2. Don't rush the proof

Under-proofed croissants will be dense and bread-like.

3. Bake them properly

A good croissant should have:

crisp, deeply browned exterior → delicate flaky layers → light, honeycomb-like interior.

🧈 One change I'd make for ingredients in India

If you can get good European-style unsalted butter, use it for the butter block. The butter quality makes a surprisingly large difference.

For the flour, strong bread flour is preferable, but a good high-protein maida can work. If using ordinary maida, don't add excessive water initially—hold back about 20–30 ml and add only if the dough needs it.

And don't substitute margarine if your goal is a proper buttery croissant. It may laminate more easily, but the flavour is noticeably different.
