---
title: 🌶️ Kerala Beef Chilli — Dry
---

1 kg beef | Serves 6
Beef
1 kg beef, cut into small 1–1½ inch pieces
1½ tsp salt
½ tsp turmeric
1½ tbsp ginger-garlic paste
1½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
1 tsp coriander powder
1 tsp freshly crushed black pepper
½ tsp garam masala
½ tsp fennel seeds, lightly crushed
½ cup water
For the final fry
3 tbsp coconut oil
2 large onions, sliced
8–10 cloves garlic, sliced/chopped
1-inch ginger, julienned
5–6 green chillies, slit
2–3 sprigs curry leaves
1 large capsicum, cut into large squares — optional
1½ tsp freshly crushed black pepper
1 tbsp soy sauce
1 tsp vinegar
1 tsp Worcestershire sauce — optional
½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
½ tsp garam masala
Salt if needed
1. Pressure-cook the beef

Put the beef into your pressure cooker with:

salt
turmeric
ginger-garlic paste
Kashmiri chilli
coriander powder
black pepper
garam masala
crushed fennel
½ cup water

Mix thoroughly.

Cook:

Full flame → first whistle → medium flame for 15–20 minutes.

Switch off and allow the pressure to release naturally.

Check the beef.

It should be tender but not falling apart.

Important:

Keep the cooking liquid.

2. Reduce the beef

Transfer the cooked beef and its liquid to a wide pan.

Cook on medium-high heat until almost all the liquid evaporates.

Keep stirring occasionally.

You want the beef to become relatively dry before the actual frying starts.

3. Fry the beef

Add 2 tbsp coconut oil.

Increase to medium-high heat.

Fry the beef for about 8–10 minutes, stirring regularly.

Let some pieces develop dark, slightly crispy edges.

Don't try to make every piece crispy—the contrast between tender and slightly crisp is ideal.

Remove the beef and keep aside.

4. Make the Kerala chilli masala

In the same pan, add another 1 tbsp coconut oil.

Add:

onions
ginger
garlic
green chillies
curry leaves

Fry on medium heat for 7–10 minutes.

You want the onions well browned, not merely translucent.

If using capsicum, add it now and stir-fry for only 2 minutes.

5. Add the chilli flavour

Lower the heat.

Add:

1½ tsp crushed black pepper
½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
½ tsp garam masala

Stir for 20–30 seconds.

Immediately add:

1 tbsp soy sauce
1 tsp vinegar
1 tsp Worcestershire sauce, if using

Mix well.

Don't add water.

6. Bring it together

Return the fried beef to the pan.

Turn the heat up.

Toss everything together for 3–5 minutes.

The sauce should completely coat the beef and then disappear into it.

Finish with another small handful of curry leaves and ½ tsp freshly crushed black pepper.

Taste for salt.

🔥 The key to Kerala Beef Chilli

The final dish should be:

dry → dark → peppery → slightly spicy → savoury → smoky

There should be no gravy at the bottom of the pan.

And I wouldn't add ketchup or sugar. They push the dish toward Indo-Chinese sweet chilli beef rather than the Kerala version.

My preferred version

I'd actually skip the capsicum for this one. You've been avoiding vegetables in several of your dishes, and I think this particular preparation is better with:

beef + onion + garlic + ginger + green chilli + curry leaves + black pepper.

The coconut oil and curry leaves will give it the Kerala character.

Best accompaniments
Kerala parotta — my first choice
Appam
Kallappam
Ghee rice
Plain matta rice

For six adults, 1 kg beef + 750 g rice is a very generous combination if you're serving rice as the main accompaniment.
